# Viral ideation

Find a worthwhile idea before choosing a catchy line. Read [craft](../viral-content/references/craft.md) and the selected [mode](../viral-content/references/modes.md). Use [formats](../viral-content/references/formats.md) when execution materially changes the concept.

## Establish the material

Use the actual audience situation, product/offer facts, and intended response. Search existing project research, customer language, ad-spy records, relevant account results, and approved prior ideas when available. For personal stories, use the speaker's own experiences. A general demonstration or explanation can use verified product facts without a founder story.

When the brief is thin, ask the question that most changes the concept: the buyer's specific struggle, the surprising product behavior, evidence the brand can show, or the decision the speaker actually made. If `grill-me` is available and the user requests an interview, use it and checkpoint each answer in its capture file. Otherwise conduct a short source-building exchange and save answers with attribution. Do not turn every request for ideas into a prolonged interview.

## Find distinct angles

Explore the useful differences: symptom versus cause; expected versus observed; two choices and their consequences; a hidden tradeoff; a demonstration that resolves doubt; an objection; an evaluator's test; a story decision; a familiar situation dramatized literally.

Assess demand, usefulness of the available answer, a convincing delivery vehicle, and differentiation. These are questions to investigate, not arbitrary numeric scores. A broad trend may reveal demand while failing to attract the relevant buyer. A narrow topic can be commercially strong even with modest reach.

For paid ideation, examine the selected account's actual winning and weak creative when authorized and accessible. Keep conversion objective, audience/placement, time window, spend, attribution, and sample limitations alongside results. Use competitor longevity or view counts to nominate hypotheses, not label profitable ads.

If current examples or customer evidence would change the choice, use [viral-research](../viral-research/SKILL.md). Search by recognizable situation, mechanism, and format as well as topic names. Do not scrape merely to decorate an already sufficient brief with citations.

## Make concepts filmable

For each candidate, connect:

- audience/situation and desired response;
- central promise or tension;
- what the viewer will see or learn;
- argument/story progression and payoff;
- an opening picture, spoken thought, and optional text;
- format and why it suits this idea;
- available proof or the material still needed.

Offer a useful number of genuinely different concepts for the request. Recommend one based on the brief and evidence, explaining the meaningful tradeoff. Cosmetic rewrites of a single premise are hook variants, not separate ideas.

Develop the substance now: the distinction to teach, decision to compare, demonstration to run, or consequence to reveal. “Your story goes here” is a sourcing note, not a finished idea. When a personal story is unavailable, propose an honest explanation, test, or illustrative scenario that the available facts support. Do not invent a founder belief; offer a clear proposition for them to accept, reject, or refine.

Reject or revise ideas that require invented results, a product-irrelevant spectacle, unintelligible inside language, or a payoff the video cannot deliver. A quiet product demonstration can beat an elaborate skit when it answers the real buying question.

Save selected concepts with IDs and source links in the project. Hand the chosen concept and constraints to [viral-script](../viral-script/SKILL.md). Keep unchosen ideas with their actual rejection reason so future work does not rediscover them as new.
