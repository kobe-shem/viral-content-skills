# Viral learning

Explain what the evidence supports and turn it into the next useful creative decision. Read [feedback.md](../viral-content/references/feedback.md). Use the bundled [feedback event log](references/feedback-cli.md) when durable records or comparisons are needed. Use the selected brand/account context; do not import another client's results.

Separate three records: what the owner/editor preferred, what the audience did, and what the business gained. Preserve the exact version, source, metric definition, time window, and unknowns. A favorable comment or owner approval cannot fill missing performance fields.

For organic work, compare the same platform/format at comparable post ages, noting audience size, topic, distribution, reposts, and known promotion. Analyze retention/share behavior separately from qualified replies or pipeline. Keyword comments may be asset requests, not buying intent.

For paid work, use actual authorized account outcomes when available. Keep spend, impressions, objective, conversion definition, attribution window, audience/placement, and delivery maturity alongside performance. Explain low-exposure uncertainty. A long-running competitor ad is a reference candidate, not a measured conversion winner. Public weak performers are weaker relative reach examples, not verified unprofitable ads.

Inspect the creative at the relevant passage. A retention drop can nominate a confusing setup, redundant beat, unmet expectation, or premature pitch; it does not independently prove the cause. Propose a focused change and state the alternative explanations.

Update project preferences from attributable owner corrections. For broader doctrine, require linked examples, scope, counterexamples, and the reason the change should generalize. Keep a previous version recoverable. Repeated claims from the same teacher are not independent replication.

Return the strongest findings with uncertainty, the next creative changes/tests, and what to measure. Avoid turning every weekly review into a new universal framework. Use a manageable number of changed variables and keep the body fixed when the intended test concerns opening variants.
