# Viral direction

Make the script work as a viewed and heard sequence. Read [craft](../viral-content/references/craft.md), the selected [format](../viral-content/references/formats.md), and [handoffs](../viral-content/references/handoffs.md). Keep the objective, script, format, style, and execution budget separate.

## Opening

Design the first visible state, action, spoken line, and exact text as one coherent event. Explain the question or expectation it creates and where the video resolves it. Account for props, actor marks, framing, camera movement, light, and the audience's ability to understand it at phone size.

Choose visual hooks from the concept: a meaningful discrepancy, visible consequence, surprising but relevant prop, interrupted routine, revealing camera position, transformation, comparison, or expressive reaction. An effect is useful because of what it makes the viewer understand or anticipate. “Add visual hooks” is not an executable instruction.

For a chosen hook, specify who/what is in frame before recording, what moves first, the camera's start/end, text placement, and its relation to speech. Keep production feasible. Offer alternatives only when they change cost, clarity, or creative strategy.

## Beat direction

Map each script beat to pictured action, performance intention, shot/framing, screen text/graphics, audio, and transition. Name its purpose: establish context, demonstrate, prove, compare, create tension, release tension, clarify, or pay off.

Plan useful restraint. A held presenter shot may sell an important line; a cut may reveal information or clarify a role change. Use B-roll to perform a job, not cover every pause. Distinguish actual proof assets from illustrations and synthetic scenes.

Maintain continuity of props, eye lines, screen direction, actor roles, UI states, and spoken argument. In claymation, preserve the mechanism across transformations. In skits, stage reactions and who knows what. In jumbotron concepts, ensure the display's message is readable and do not imply a real public endorsement or event placement unless true.

Keep text and captions distinct: a persistent title can establish context while captions follow actual speech. Reserve space for face/hands/product and placement UI; inspect actual layouts when available. Specify entrances, holds, and exits by narrative cue before actual timing exists.

## Reference-based style

Use `video-style-study` when a creator's measured visual/editing style is requested and that skill is installed. Study coherent formats with strong, ordinary, and weak examples. Look at visual hooks, speech/pauses, reveal timing, camera movement, text hierarchy, cuts, B-roll, music, SFX, and the closing hold. Teacher claims and reference observations remain separate.

Reference footage is for study unless reuse rights are established. A transcript does not establish visual blocking; sampled frames do not establish pacing, sound, or lip sync. Record actual modality coverage and leave unavailable observations unresolved. Never mark a whole style “learned” based on captions or a contact sheet.

## Editing and generation handoff

Produce an editor-neutral beat plan with script/claim versions, cues, required assets, evidence, and unresolved production choices. Timings are estimated until footage is measured. Use existing generation skills for missing footage when requested; preserve approved script and identity/claim constraints.

When `video-edit` is installed and editing is requested, pass the plan into its ingest and single-master-timeline workflow. Actual source timings and eligible media must exist before compiling its `edit.json`. Do not emit a superficially complete timeline with invented source IDs or hashes.

If no editing skill/runtime is installed, the deliverable remains a complete directing and editor handoff document. Name the missing capability without pretending a render happened. A completed video requires audiovisual review through the editing workflow; a directing brief alone is not a completed video.
