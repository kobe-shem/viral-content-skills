# Viral scripting

Write the requested words so they can be spoken and filmed. For a body-only rewrite, start with the short [body revision procedure](references/body-rewrite.md) and the supplied brief; load additional research only for an actual gap. For new full scripts, read [craft](../viral-content/references/craft.md), the applicable [mode](../viral-content/references/modes.md), and relevant [examples](../viral-content/references/examples.md). Consult source-specific detail only when it changes a decision.

## Lock the job

Identify audience, objective, awareness, scope, creative format, speaker, duration if supplied, and the facts/claims/CTA that must remain accurate. Use the supplied brief and project profile. Do not substitute another brand's voice or force a first-person founder story onto a client ad.

Check explicit prior decisions before rewriting. If the topic itself was rejected, removing private details or changing its hook does not make it approved. Develop a different premise within the brief. Distinguish a rejected topic, rejected wording, and rejected format; preserve the parts the user liked.

Honor full-script requests with exact wording. Use speaking cards when requested or supported by that speaker's actual preference: exact opening/payoff and substantive talking points that explain what to discuss. Four-word topic labels are not useful speaking notes.

For a body-only rewrite, establish what the existing hook has already promised. Develop that argument and preserve the supplied ending/offer boundary. Do not re-open the video or add a fresh CTA automatically. If the hook is unavailable, write a body that fits the supplied context and briefly state the assumption when consequential.

## Build the argument before polishing

Privately outline the movement: what the opening makes the viewer expect, what changes in each beat, and what pays it off. Choose the shape for this brief; do not force every script into a fixed number of beats.

For cold paid ads, consider Perdriau's problem education → mechanism/category → product bridge. Make the product a logical answer to the situation. For aware audiences, direct product visibility, comparison, or an offer can be the right opening. Use Hormozi's relevance/value test for hooks and distinct hook variants around a stable body where testing benefits from that control.

For stories, give enough context to understand the decision, then progress through the meaningful change and consequence. For demonstrations, show the result or question early and explain the useful steps. For skits, characters pursue goals and react; they should not recite alternating marketing bullet points.

Make the consequence tangible. A useful sequence is a recognizable moment, what changes, what that forces the person to do, and how the solution changes that choice. Use it when it fits the situation. Merely naming a pain and then announcing the brand is not a developed argument. Illustrative situations can make supplied facts concrete; label invented demonstrations as such and never present them as actual customer events or proof.

Place proof and objection handling where the audience needs them. Avoid introducing an objection that the brief's audience is unlikely to have. If evidence is absent, write a supportable claim or identify the required source; don't fill the gap with invented certainty.

## Draft for speech and picture

Use ordinary language, concrete actions, and the relevant buyer's vocabulary. Vary sentence length for the thought, not for a manufactured rhythm. Preserve necessary transitions. Remove artificial metaphors, symmetrical lists, forced punchlines, repetitive problem statements, and vague claims where they weaken this script.

When a picture communicates the fact, let the words add meaning instead of narrating the obvious. Coordinate the opening spoken line, exact screen text, and visible action. Specify directions separately from spoken copy. Do not insert source citations or production metadata into the dialogue.

Avoid universal bans on questions, “how to” hooks, emotional language, long sentences, or particular words. Those choices succeed or fail in context. The test is comprehension, relevance, credibility, progression, and delivery.

## Review the actual draft

Check the opening promise against the final payoff. Look for the first useful reward; the passage most likely to lose the intended viewer; whether the product bridge follows the argument; and whether every claim is supported. Read the words as speech, checking breath, awkward phrasing, and whether directions are physically possible.

Run a redundancy pass on the actual sentences. If a sentence only rephrases an established payment gap, conflict, concern or benefit, remove it unless it supplies necessary comprehension or emphasis. Developing a body means adding a useful consequence, explanation, distinction or evidence; it does not necessarily mean lengthening it. Prefer the shorter passage when it preserves the argument and sounds natural.

Treat word-count runtime as an estimate. Recordings determine delivery speed, pauses, and final timing. Do not label an unrecorded script as measured or performance-tested.

Budget physical actions and reactions alongside speech. Do not fit eight sequential actions into a five-second reveal by assigning optimistic timestamps. Simplify the choreography or lengthen the planned window. In skits, check every action against the character's goal; a character should not deliberately make a nonsensical choice just to demonstrate a feature.

For hook variants, change the audience entry, proof, tension, or visual idea. Rank against the brief, not a generic “viral score.” Preserve the body and material facts when the test is intended to isolate the hook.

## Deliver and hand off

Give the usable script first. Add the exact screen hook/physical opening when requested or necessary to make it work. Briefly explain a consequential structural change; skip routine self-evaluation. For full production, pass the script and selected format to [viral-direct](../viral-direct/SKILL.md) using [handoffs](../viral-content/references/handoffs.md).

Store rejected/current versions and actual user corrections in the project. For personal taste, update that brand/speaker profile; for a reproducible general failure, nominate a scoped example or rule update. Acceptance by a writer or owner is not evidence of audience performance.
