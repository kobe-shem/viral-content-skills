# Rewrite only the body

A body already follows an opening and may lead into a locked ending. Work within that boundary. Preserve the brief's offer, qualification and claim scope. Output only the copy when requested.

First identify the existing movement: situation → consequence or useful distinction → relevant product behavior → supplied qualification/next-step information. If that movement already works, edit for natural speech and clarity. Do not expand it simply to demonstrate that a doctrine was applied.

Make the two sides of a comparison easy to hear. Concrete deadlines, actions and roles usually help more than additional explanation. A repeated definition or a polished slogan can weaken an otherwise clear passage.

When a logical step is missing, add the minimum useful connection. When it is present, preserve it. Do not narrate the viewer's reasoning twice or add a sentence that announces the need immediately before the product sentence explains the same need.

Constructed example:

> Your meeting starts in five minutes, and someone else is already in the room. Now you have to find another space or move the call. RoomClock shows everyone the same availability and blocks a slot once it is booked.

Adding “This is why everyone needs a shared place to check availability before making plans” between the consequence and product would repeat the bridge. It does not add persuasion.

For a trust question, move from the specific check to the supplied evidence and the viewer's decision. “Read other customers' reviews. You can consider what those customers say about their experience” repeats itself. Do not replace the second sentence with a different repetition.

Read the final copy as one spoken passage. Prefer an existing strong sentence over a newly invented weaker one. Keep necessary nuance, such as an exemption applying only to a subset of transactions. Do not turn an application into an approval, a network into a direct provider, or an example into a guarantee.

If useful, privately compare a close edit against a developed alternative and keep the stronger passage. The user needs one usable body, not a visible review form or automatic list of variants. Source detail and review status stay outside the spoken copy.
