# Feedback and experiment records

Keep lightweight records in the project. Use the provided CLI/schema when helpful; a short task should not become an administrative exercise.

## Editorial feedback

Record asset/script revision and hash, the actual source of feedback, exact requested change or preference, accepted/rejected/revised status, and scope: this line, this format, this speaker/brand, or a proposed general rule. Save both sides when the owner supplies a rewrite.

Editorial failure examples worth testing include an irrelevant hook, repeated problem statements, missing causal transition, product pitch before the problem is understood, vague speaking notes, unnatural balanced clauses, visual confusion, an unfulfilled promise, and loss of requested scope. These are diagnosis options, not mandatory defects in every draft.

Never infer approval from silence. A model's preference is labeled model editorial judgment and cannot be attributed to the owner.

## Organic snapshots

Record platform, post ID/URL, publication and capture times, age, mode, format, topic, hook/variant, views/impressions/reach as separately defined, duration, watch metrics, shares/saves/comments when available, and qualified responses. Missing metrics are null, not zero. A 24-hour and seven-day snapshot can make comparison easier; use the platform's actual available windows.

Document what “qualified” means for this business and what evidence supports it. A one-word comment requesting a download is not automatically a qualified inquiry. Attributed pipeline requires an actual opportunity and a source/attribution method.

## Paid snapshots

Record account/campaign/ad IDs in the private project, objective, period, attribution setting, currency, spend, impressions, conversion event/count, conversion value if available, and derived rates with denominators. Separate creative changes from targeting, placement, landing-page, offer, and delivery changes.

Use enough exposure for the decision being made; no universal spend or conversion threshold fits every account. Flag immature or low-volume observations. Distinguish no recorded conversions from missing conversion tracking. Revenue and ROAS cannot be inferred from clicks or leads.

## Compare and test

Choose comparable cases and state exclusions before interpreting results. Keep exact duplicates and near-duplicate variants linked. Analyze account-relative performance within a sensible same-format/time cohort; lifetime views over a tiny baseline are not a stable measure of creative lift.

For a focused hook test, keep the body, offer, and landing experience stable when feasible. For a concept test, label that several creative features change together. A platform's nonrandom delivery can still confound conclusions.

Save the hypothesis, changed variable(s), expected audience behavior, primary metric, decision window, failure interpretation, and resulting next action. Distinguish confirmed measurements, plausible explanations, and proposed experiments.

## Update rules

Promote a reusable rule only with its conditions, evidence, exceptions, and contrary examples. Retain failed tests and old versions. A single successful post can justify a sibling test without becoming a permanent content policy. Revisit source freshness and applicability when audience, offer, platform, or format changes.
