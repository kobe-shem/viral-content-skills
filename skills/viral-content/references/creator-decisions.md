# What to use from each creator

Use these as decision references. They combine attributed teaching with explicitly scoped observations, not endorsements or universal performance laws. Full source media and course transcripts belong in the research workspace. Dates and sample coverage are recorded in the repository research report.

## Nathan Perdriau — the paid argument

In [his 2026 creative system](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vUbLw80KTpo&t=1564s), 26:04 onward, he separates the audience/persona, angle and offer from execution. At 48:08–51:20 he treats a hook as a relevance promise, with visual, audio and copy layers. He explicitly qualifies hook-volume advice on having a good body. At 64:52–67:50 he chooses format by education, single-frame clarity, demonstration, awareness and production economics.

**Apply:** make concept variation substantive; choose a recognizable buying situation; develop the body before multiplying hooks. Match production cost to the question the creative must answer.

**Observed example:** [This Is a Bad Ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KG61is5_egU) keeps the critiqued UGC above Nathan's commentary. At roughly 00:06–00:14 he challenges why a stranger should care about the speaker's supplement routine; later he recognizes useful education and reasonable objection handling. The split screen makes the evidence available during the verdict. This is a teacher critique containing another brand's ad, not proof of Nathan's own ad-account return.

**Exception:** aware audiences, recognizable products and actual demonstrations may work with immediate product visibility. His critique of one early pitch is not a blanket product-hiding rule.

## Alex Hormozi — promises, rewards and modular ad production

[Mozi Media Content Method Pt I](https://www.acquisition.com/training/leads5), 05:47–06:28, defines the content unit as attracting attention, retaining it until value arrives, and rewarding the original reason for watching. At 18:03–21:49 he develops lists, ordered steps and stories as retention structures. At 22:02–23:14 he warns against endlessly opening loops without rewards and emphasizes value per second rather than shortness alone. At 24:26–27:44 he reconnects reward to the opening promise and describes longer work as multiple connected content units. Source timing is recovered ASR and approximate.

The recovered *Hooks* playbook (source PDF pages 6–8; printed pages 3–5) defines attention through relevant callout and expected value, using words, picture and sound. *GOATed Ads* (source pages 8–15; printed 6–13) separates hooks, bodies and CTAs and matches creative to awareness. Its source pages 16–22 include demonstration, testimonial, education, story and faceless examples; the same argument can take multiple forms.

**Apply:** write a body that fulfills the hook; test compatible openings against a stable argument. Check hook/body/CTA combinations for continuity before multiplying them. A different hook does not automatically create a compatible promise.

**Observed example:** [Follow your dreams](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/c6EGmxjMpPw) moves from a provocative quotation to a lived-business account, then distinguishes enjoying fitness from earning through it. Sampled frames show presenter-led opening, archival/gym footage during the experience, and a return to the presenter for the conclusion. Use authentic evidence when the story claims experience. Do not borrow his biography or scale.

**Exception:** his production-volume and allocation ratios describe his operation. Set output volume from meaningful concepts, available proof, production capacity and test exposure.

## Ava Yuergens / Personal Brand Launch — hook combinations and visual teaching

[Hooks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sRutnMnQGo4), 00:16–00:32 and 06:32–07:44, separates spoken, written and pictured openings. Her examples coordinate labels with comparison/cloning visuals. [Scripting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJWWGq_Yuoo), 03:05–04:05, extracts structural roles before replacing the content; 04:53–06:36 distinguishes simple reach formats from deeper formats she reports generating more followers.

[Storytelling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yQX9wg5kvCA), 06:37–08:29, pairs each spoken line with needed filming and editing assets, using real historical footage when available. Treat her exact cut-frequency suggestions as style options.

**Observed example:** [Profile optimization](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ChifKqh25Nk) alternates a casual couch presenter with high-contrast screenshots and before/after instruction. At 00:26–00:40 the picture supplies the comparison being discussed; 01:31–01:47 reveals a bio structure progressively. This is long-form teaching evidence, not a proxy for her Instagram opening style.

**Apply:** design complementary hook channels, then script the complete informational progression and attach shot/edit cues to it.

**Exception:** her views-to-followers discovery filter is a heuristic. It does not establish an account-relative performance lift, paid conversion or causal transfer. Preserve creator/date/format baselines separately.

## Adley Kinsman — visual questions with a visible resolution

In [her interview with Social Media Examiner](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tcxfKwZwhsA), the chaptered discussion treats the opening (13:07), suspense (24:47), payoff (32:48), staging/props (40:59) and testing (46:07) as connected creative decisions. Check the speaker and specific passage before attributing a detailed rule.

**Observed example:** [Tortilla record](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5fJTy6VMHLI) makes the question physical: an ordinary tortilla is put on a record player, fails, undergoes escalating preparation, then produces a surprising playback reaction. Sampled frames corroborate the record-player experiment, microwave, rolling/drilling and final reaction; scene analysis supplies secondary timing/audio observations. The object stays recognizable through the sequence, so each action advances the same expectation.

**Apply:** reveal the discrepancy quickly; show progress and setbacks; hold the decisive action long enough to understand the outcome. Build the final picture while designing the opening.

**Exception:** entertaining staged transformation is not technical product proof. For a paid demo, the product must actually explain and support the depicted result. Copying only the bizarre prop risks attracting spectators with no interest in the offer.

## Daniel Iles / Viral Coach — natural delivery and an explicit funnel job

[His free training](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hp328yUV6O8), 67:30–73:10, distinguishes acquisition, nurturing and conversion jobs, then compares exact scripts with flexible speaking points. He stresses hearing the words aloud before filming and choosing a format the speaker can deliver naturally. His self-reported client comparisons are teaching evidence, not independently verified trials.

**Observed example:** [The best-performing ads](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/SddOgMRZosc) opens with an attention-first proposition, explains the distracted viewing situation, and progresses to later marketing/retargeting. The sampled footage uses a plain seated presenter, changing crop, expressive gestures and selectively enlarged caption words. A model's six semantic segments are not evidence of six cuts. The final argumentative statement is not a CTA.

**Apply:** define the ad's immediate job, write in the speaker's voice, and let gesture/caption emphasis carry relationships such as “first” and “then.” Simple staging can support a developed argument.

**Exception:** cheap attention is insufficient for a conversion objective. An attention-first campaign needs evidence that the acquired audience and later funnel produce valuable outcomes after all costs. Reconcile this with Nathan's relevance test instead of adopting “ads should never sell.”
