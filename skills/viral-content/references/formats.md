# Creative format decisions

Use these as execution options, not a required menu or a taxonomy that excludes new ideas. Record the objective and argument before selecting the format. A user-selected format remains the starting constraint.

| Format | How it carries the idea | Opening/directing choices | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct-to-camera / UGC-style presenter | Recognition, explanation, credible demonstration, personal story when real | Relevant object/action already in frame; intentional eye line; text establishes context; speech begins on the thought | Synthetic presenter invents personal experience; staged spontaneity; abstract monologue with unrelated B-roll |
| Claymation / stop-motion illustration | Makes an abstract mechanism physical through tactile cause and effect | Clear scale, one prop transformation, strong silhouette, legible labels; narration explains only what motion cannot | Attractive motion has no causal connection to the offer; metaphor mistaken for evidence |
| Skit / dialogue | Dramatizes competing goals, assumptions, or consequences | Distinct roles and geography, immediate conflict, readable reaction, escalation through decisions | Characters explain the marketing brief to each other; interchangeable voices; joke and product are unrelated |
| Jumbotron / public-display illusion | Makes the message conspicuous in a recognizable environment | Establish display and message quickly; credible perspective/reflections; motivated camera reveal; readable content | Viewer spends the whole opening decoding the setup; implied real sponsorship/event/endorsement; display text too small |
| Demonstration / proof-led | Shows a result, process, contrast, or objection being resolved | Open on result or test setup; show the relevant input/output; preserve causally necessary steps | Synthetic UI presented as actual product evidence; unexplained screen; result never shown |
| Teardown / reaction | Shows judgment by interpreting a visible example | Source visible; explicit evaluation question; annotations point to the exact evidence | Narrator repeats the clip; source overwhelms commentary; popularity treated as correctness |
| Comparison / levels | Makes a meaningful difference easy to see | Constant comparison axis; readable labels; same task under each condition | Unfair comparison, invented before/after result, three labels with no demonstrated difference |
| Story / founder narrative | Earns an insight through a decision and consequence | Open near the tension; enough context for stakes; concrete scene/action | Biography before relevance; manufactured trauma; moral stated repeatedly |
| Silent text over footage | Makes a concise observation or story readable without speech | One readable initial thought; footage supports mood or meaning; timed text progression | Walls of text, arbitrary duration, caption transcript treated as spoken copy |
| Whiteboard / worked example | Helps the audience understand a decision or mechanism | Start with the question/result; legible numbers; reveal steps in order | Fictional math masquerades as actual results; excessive setup; unreadable diagram |
| Product/offer comparison ad | Resolves buying uncertainty for aware prospects | Product and comparison criterion appear early; genuine difference visible | Forced delayed brand reveal; unsupported superiority; omitted comparison conditions |

## Physical hook specification

Name actor, prop, initial position, first visible action, camera position/movement, spoken/text relationship, and what question the action creates. For example, an owner reaches for inventory on a shelf while a marked credit-limit strip visibly retracts. That is an illustrative concept; it does not establish what a particular bank did.

Do not prescribe a strange angle or a walk into frame without a narrative reason. The visual must still read on a small screen within the intended opening window. If a prop, label, or hand blocks the product or face, fix staging before adding graphics.

## Screen hook specification

Supply exact text; its role (context, comparison, contradiction, evidence, or question); placement relative to face/hands/product; entrance and hold; and any change at payoff. Check actual placement overlays rather than assuming a single safe-zone template works everywhere. Avoid a universal word-count rule: readability depends on font, size, contrast, motion, and exposure time.

## Sound and delivery

Describe intention and an actionable performance: controlled disbelief, one pause before the reveal, a turn toward the prop, a quiet correction. “Be authentic” is insufficient. Decide whether music/SFX improve the scene; silence can be purposeful. Do not claim a reference's musical feeling or lip sync was reviewed from still images.

## Novel formats

Derive a new format from its narrative engine, visual grammar, production requirements, and failure risks. Keep the sales/educational argument visible beneath the execution. A format learned from organic references can be tested in paid work, but its paid performance remains unestablished until measured.
