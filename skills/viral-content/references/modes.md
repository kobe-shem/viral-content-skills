# Objective, awareness, scope, and format

Resolve these axes independently. The same creator and visual style can serve different objectives.

| Mode | Main job | Good opening | Body/payoff | Primary outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organic reach | Earn attention, usefulness, sharing, or entertainment from a chosen audience | Recognizable tension, meaningful surprise, visible result, story problem | Delivers the promised insight, reveal, method, or resolution | Comparable retention, shares/saves, relevant follows; raw reach separately |
| Organic authority | Help a likely buyer understand or decide; demonstrate judgment | Specific situation, diagnostic question, case, worked example | Useful answer, credible reasoning/proof, next step when relevant | Qualified questions, useful replies, attributable pipeline; views separately |
| Paid conversion | Help an appropriate prospect take a real next step | Buyer-relevant problem, product demonstration, comparison, credible offer | Persuasive argument with mechanism, evidence, relevant objections, and an accurate CTA | Account-defined conversion and economics within an attribution window |

“Organic” does not mean no business purpose. “Paid” does not mean shout benefits immediately. A reach post can omit a CTA; a product-aware ad can lead with the offer. Do not grade every mode by the same metric.

## Awareness changes the argument

- **Unaware:** make the recognizable symptom and consequence legible before teaching a new category.
- **Problem-aware:** clarify the cause or decision the viewer has not resolved. Avoid spending the whole ad restating their pain.
- **Solution-aware:** compare mechanisms, explain an objection, or show the solution in use.
- **Product-aware:** demonstrate relevant proof, fit, differentiation, or a reason to act. Brand/product can appear immediately.
- **Most-aware:** make the actual offer, terms, and next step easy to understand.

Awareness is a working hypothesis about the audience, not a fact inferred from a platform label. Existing ad-account or customer evidence can refine it. When unavailable, explain the consequential assumption briefly.

## Scope preservation

- **Hook only:** deliver distinct opening approaches that can connect to the supplied body. Include visual/text counterparts when requested or materially useful.
- **Body only:** begin where the existing hook leaves the viewer. Preserve the offer and requested boundaries; do not add a new cold open or CTA unless needed and requested.
- **Full script:** supply the exact words. Use a duration estimate, not a fabricated measured runtime. A read-aloud recording establishes actual duration.
- **Speaking card:** exact opening/payoff plus sufficiently descriptive talking points. This is a selected recording interface, not a universal default for every speaker.
- **Rewrite:** identify the core problem, then make the change. Do not preserve a weak argument merely by replacing adjectives.
- **Direction/edit revision:** preserve approved words unless the edit exposes a material clarity or timing problem. Surface that change explicitly.

## Brand context

Use a project-local context file or the supplied brief for audience, offer, supported claims, CTA, speaker, voice examples, taboo claims, and actual research/analytics locations. The portable skill has no preferred client, niche, vocabulary, approval history, or mandatory first-person origin.

Personal experience requires the speaker's own source. A brand explanation can rely on verified product/offer facts without inventing a founder anecdote. Do not import unrelated client results or credentials from the example library.

For paid work, keep supplied eligibility criteria and material offer terms intact. A rewrite must not change what the audience can actually receive or imply approval, savings, credit effects, or performance unsupported by the brief.
