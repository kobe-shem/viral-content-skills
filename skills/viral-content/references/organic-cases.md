# Organic cases: bounded retrospective comparisons

Captured 2026-09-06. These cases compare public Instagram posts inside bounded creator-relative
cohorts. Rank labels and view counts were visible before annotation, so this is retrospective
forensics rather than a blind prediction test. A view gap can identify a contrast worth testing;
it cannot prove which craft choice caused the gap. Audience size at publication, boost status,
distribution, retention, qualified response, and conversion are unknown.

For every case below, the private evidence packet contains the retrieved video and audio, a local
word-timed `small.en` ASR transcript, frame zero, and an eight-frame contact sheet. All 15 contact
sheets and all 15 opening/body/ending transcripts were inspected. ASR wording and boundaries are
approximate. Music and sound design were not independently coded, except that Ava's sound-effects
case visibly and verbally presents named sound categories.

## Nathan Perdriau: the same accumulating tier-list format

Cohort screen: organic Instagram video, 2026-06-14 through 2026-07-20, 40–80 seconds. There were 93
eligible posts before the visual-format screen. The selected cases all use Nathan centered in a
dark studio, a persistent tier grid in the lower half, burned captions, and reference thumbnails
that accumulate on the grid. Their overall cohort ranks are 92/93, 91/93, and 38/93. The third is a
lower result in the repeated tier-list family, not a channel-wide floor.

- **High — [metrics that matter for e-commerce](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZrCOSfEVi-/),
  297,792 plays, 2026-06-17, 70.5s.** The spoken opening immediately names a metric and assigns it
  to the top tier; frame one adds a broad “what to fix before scaling” wrapper. Each body beat
  names a metric, places its thumbnail, gives a short judgment, and advances the visible grid.
  Several beats add a consequence or comparison rather than stopping at the label. The ending
  closes the inventory with a stock-depth caveat; there is no ask. The visual payoff is cumulative:
  the empty grid becomes a completed decision map.
- **Second higher-reach reference — [checkout interventions](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZjAisavZSH/), 91,982 plays,
  2026-06-14, 79.3s.** The video starts directly with Shop Pay and Apple Pay rather than a spoken
  thesis. It runs the same name → rank → reason loop across checkout tactics. The body makes
  several judgments conditional on margin, brand fit, industry, or operating cost. It ends on
  the final live-chat ranking without a recap or CTA. The grid supplies progress and closure even
  though the speech is a list.
- **Lower format-family result — [offer types by first-purchase profitability](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbAd1Gyk_F7/),
  5,290 plays, 2026-07-20, 46.8s.** The opening names the decision criterion and immediately begins
  ranking. The body keeps the same grid, captions, and evidence-card motion, but covers five dense
  offer economics in less time. It closes on two negative rankings with no synthesis or CTA.
  Because topic, density, and publication date differ, the lower reach does not show that the
  tier-list structure failed.

Observed across all three: the format does not depend on a delayed narrative reveal. Retention is
carried by rapid judgments, recurring micro-payoffs, and visible completion. The evidence supports
using a persistent progress object for evaluative content. It does not show that grids cause reach,
or that Nathan's business-specific terminology transfers to a broad audience.

## Alex Hormozi: static dark-background answers

Cohort screen: organic Instagram video, 2026-08-01 through 2026-08-03, 40–60 seconds. Exactly three
retrieved posts met those date and duration constraints; they rank 3/3, 2/3, and 1/3 by play count.
All use a largely static dark-background speaker frame with burned captions, but the source shoots,
wardrobe, and question context differ.

- **High — [young founder objection](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dbi211wuFyV/), 760,303 plays,
  2026-08-02, 45.6s.** A young founder's concrete problem opens the clip; a red written pull-quote
  sharpens the objection. Hormozi answers with a personal story about hiding ownership behind an
  imagined boss. The body advances by specific actions and reactions. The ending resolves the
  original objection with a brief “use this” handoff. The reward is an executable workaround
  carried by a compact story.
- **Middle — [“trapped” in a low-wage job](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dbe0zV3hNEI/), 540,403 plays,
  2026-08-01, 59.9s.** The opening preserves the audience question, then immediately challenges
  its loaded word. The body separates the international-selling question from the belief behind
  it and uses concrete market size and a simple business definition. The final turn reframes low
  wages as making replacement income easier. There is no CTA; the last reframing is the payoff.
- **Low — [certainty before starting](https://www.instagram.com/p/DblsOd_hP-v/), 135,150 plays,
  2026-08-03, 47.4s.** The opening states an abstract rule and adds a red written promise that
  certainty will never arrive. The body uses Colonel Sanders as an exception, then moves to goals,
  action, risk tolerance, and endurance. The ending restates the uncertainty thesis. It supplies
  an argumentative close rather than a practical next step.

The strongest case in this three-post window begins with a specific person's problem and pays it
off with a story-based tactic. The lowest begins with a general principle and closes on the same
principle. That is a useful specificity hypothesis for testing. Three posts cannot establish it,
and the middle case also opens with a specific question while using a different payoff type.

## Ava Yuergens / Personal Brand Launch: one outdoor visual system

Cohort screen: organic Instagram video, 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31, 40–80 seconds. Forty-two
posts met the date and duration rules. The selected cases rank 42/42, 23/42, and 6/42. All three
use an outdoor presenter, lime-and-white text and floating examples. The higher-reach case uses a brown deck/red railing with both hands open; the other two use a gray deck/white railing and handheld microphone. This is a related visual family, not an identical production setup.

- **High — [Sound Effects 101](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZXUbeGPBWp/), 3,565,818 plays,
  2026-06-09, 61.3s.** Frame one, spoken words, and the visual field all introduce sound-effect
  categories. The persistent title stays while labeled examples accumulate around Ava. The body
  is a catalog of named categories and demonstrations, so each beat gives an immediate sensory
  payoff while promising another. The ending offers the categorized resource through a comment
  keyword. This case visibly supports aligned written, spoken, and demonstrative hooks.
- **Middle — [talking head versus “yap”](https://www.instagram.com/p/DaXsUa1j6a0/), 76,689 plays,
  2026-07-04, 67.6s.** The opening says the two formats while the top labels, examples, and Ava's
  left/right pointing establish the same comparison visually. The body first defines the two
  formats, then splits the second into opinion and structured-list variants. Floating examples
  and attributes fill the branches as she speaks. The ending names both as currently useful and
  asks viewers to follow. The payoff is a completed taxonomy.
- **Low — [following versus personal brand](https://www.instagram.com/p/DaiA1H9hd0i/), 41,605
  plays, 2026-07-08, 41.1s.** A persistent two-column header, thumbnail examples, spoken contrasts,
  and pointing gestures all communicate the comparison from the opening. The body runs a fast
  series of paired distinctions. It ends on the last classification without a recap or audible
  CTA. Its hook channels are aligned; the lower result is therefore a counterexample to treating
  alignment alone as sufficient.

Across these cases, the house style remains recognizable at both ends of the rank range. The large
gap is compatible with several hypotheses: the high case offers audible demonstrations and a
downloadable resource, while the other two offer taxonomies. The sample does not isolate sound,
topic demand, keyword comments, or distribution as the cause.

## Adley Kinsman: closest topic-duration cohort, production context unmatched

Cohort screen: organic Instagram video, 2026-06-09 through 2026-06-21, 30–45 seconds. Four posts
met the date and duration rules; the selected cases rank 4/4, 2/4, and 1/4. All concern attention
or content, but they come from a group conversation, a product-demo-to-interview edit, and a stage
talk. This is not a clean visual-format match, so use it to compare mechanisms and to identify the
next matched collection need.

- **High — [extreme things done for content](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZ2g4rgMvQt/), 903,217
  plays, 2026-06-21, 43.3s.** The opening begins inside a provocative confession while the written
  frame labels the topic as the most extreme content decisions. The body stacks four escalating,
  concrete anecdotes: a disguise, deleted footage recovered into revenue, a store ban, and hired
  actors. Each anecdote supplies its own surprise or punch line; the last resolves with a two-word
  result. There is no CTA. Retention comes from escalating specificity and repeated mini-payoffs.
- **Middle — [spike-cover demo into attention lesson](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZt3R_Whgk_/),
  208,983 plays, 2026-06-18, 31.8s.** The first eleven seconds show another creator naming a track
  problem, fitting the cover, and demonstrating the result. The edit then reveals Adley in an
  interview, where she uses the example to argue that attention can be engineered. It closes with
  a comment-keyword offer. The source shows another creator's product demonstration, not an independently verified performance test. Its use in Adley's post is a borrowed example that bridges into her service pitch.
- **Low — [entertainment versus stated preference](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZYkPMiP32f/),
  57,552 plays, 2026-06-09, 32.1s.** A stage excerpt opens on a general marketing distinction. The
  body contrasts what people say they want with habitual attention, using a cat-video example and
  a dopamine explanation. It ends by restating that content must meet the entertainment context.
  Captions follow the speech; the stage and body position remain stable. There is no narrative
  reveal or CTA inside the retrieved clip.

The high case supplies multiple surprising events; the low case explains an attention belief.
That contrast supports testing enacted examples against abstract instruction. It does not prove
the mechanism because the production settings, speakers, and distribution context differ.

## Daniel Iles: dark-studio business monologues

Cohort screen: organic Instagram video, 2026-06-01 through 2026-08-15, 50–75 seconds. Thirty-four
posts met the date and duration rules. The selected cases rank 34/34, 17/34, and 1/34. All are
dark-studio talking-head monologues with kinetic captions and occasional full-frame text or visual
evidence. The high and low share the closest black-curtain desk setup; the middle uses a different
dark studio and a handheld microphone.

- **High — [contextual ad targeting](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbbNDvykeDR/), 61,836 plays,
  2026-07-30, 56.3s.** The opening states a restriction and immediately promises a remaining way
  to achieve the outcome. The body gives a short policy backstory, withholds the name until about
  24 seconds, then defines contextual targeting and explains how speech and visuals signal the
  intended audience. Large keywords and white interstitial cards punctuate the monologue. The
  ending turns the explanation into one action: say things uniquely relevant to the desired
  audience. No CTA follows. The opening policy claim is attributed to the speaker and has not been verified as a current platform rule.
- **Middle — [getting vendors to build faster](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZgzYw9iIVh/), 5,945
  plays, 2026-06-13, 59.5s.** The opening makes a broad claim about advantages at higher revenue.
  The body explains vendor incentives, then adds a concession that the tactic also worked when
  the business was small. Sparse white text cards break up the stable talking head. The ending
  resolves the argument through reciprocity: help a vendor learn and the vendor has reason to
  help you. There is no CTA.
- **Low — [buying customers on demand](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbraMDFgATF/), 2,926 plays,
  2026-08-06, 69.1s.** The opening names a valuable business skill and follows within four seconds
  with a large ad-spend claim as proof. The body contrasts warm referrals with the harder cold-
  traffic task, using large keywords and white interstitial cards. It closes by connecting cold
  acquisition to a scalable offer. There is no ask. This lower case contains the promise, early
  proof, argument, and closing reward often recommended in teaching, so those components are not
  sufficient to predict reach.

Daniel's high case opens a concrete information gap and delays the named mechanism while explaining
why it exists. The low case opens with proof and a broad business promise. This is evidence for
testing information-gap specificity against authority-first openings within his house style. It is
not evidence that early proof harms performance.

## What these cases can safely change in the workflow

1. Require the writer/director to name the content's reward type: completed map, executable tactic,
   resolved story, demonstrated result, taxonomy, or final argument. A closing statement is not
   automatically a CTA.
2. Keep written, spoken, and physical opening channels semantically aligned, then test the strength
   of the promised experience. Ava's lower case shows that alignment can be present without a high
   outcome.
3. Use progress objects for list and evaluation formats. Nathan's tier grids make completion visible
   and deliver a micro-payoff on every placement.
4. Distinguish demonstration from illustration. Adley's spike-cover excerpt depicts a product-use demonstration; neither it nor an entertaining transformation independently verifies a technical claim. Preserve whose product and footage are shown.
5. Preserve counterexamples. Daniel's lowest case contains early proof and a logical payoff;
   Hormozi's lowest contains a clear thesis; Nathan's lower tier-list uses the same visual system.
   Do not convert any one recommended component into a guaranteed-performance rule.
6. Run the next comparison blind. Match account, era, duration, source format, topic family, and
   distribution status where available; hide outcome rank until opening, body, reward, visual,
   delivery, and audio annotations are complete.
